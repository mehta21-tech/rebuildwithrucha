The two nutrients nobody counted

01 — The gap

0.6

Grams of protein per kg

Roughly what a typical Indian adult eats. The evidence supports 1.0 to 1.2 for a healthy adult, and more if you are over fifty, training, or losing fat rather than weight.

4g

Protein in a katori of dal

Not twenty. Four to five. Dal is a good food carrying a reputation it could never support, and that one misunderstanding underwrites most Indian protein deficits.

1990s

When the ghee left

Swapped for refined seed oils on advice that has since been heavily revised. Fat carries vitamins A, D, E and K, keeps you full, and is what makes vegetables get eaten at all.

Four rules that do the work

03 — The method

Rule 01

Thirty grams of protein before noon

The Indian breakfast is the weakest protein meal in the world and it sets up everything that follows. Fix the morning and the eleven o'clock biscuit disappears without anybody using willpower.

Rule 02

Put the fat back

Ghee on the roti, malai in the curd, coconut in the chutney, peanuts in the poha. Fat is what makes a meal hold you for four hours instead of two, and it is how the vitamins in your sabzi get absorbed at all.

Rule 03

Dal is a side, not the protein

Every meal needs a named protein that is not dal: curd, paneer, egg, soya, chana, fish, chicken. This single change is worth more than every carbohydrate rule combined.

Rule 04

Change nothing else

Same kitchen, same cook, same festivals, same wedding season. If a plan cannot survive a Gujarati household in Shravan, it is not a plan, it is a holiday.

Questions

09 — FAQ

I'm vegetarian. Can I actually hit these numbers?

Yes, and roughly two thirds of my clients are. Curd, milk, paneer, soya, chana, rajma and peanuts do it without any powder. It takes more planning than a non-vegetarian diet, which is exactly what the programme is for. Jain clients get a separate framework built without the restricted vegetables.

I have a diagnosed condition. Can you still work with me?

Often yes, but not on the condition itself. I can help with general eating, protein adequacy and habits, as long as that does not cut across what your doctor or dietitian has told you. Where the food advice is part of treating the condition, that belongs with them and I will refer you. Plenty of my clients see both of us.

Do you give meal plans?

Not printed seven-day menus, no. Those work for about eleven days and then the wedding season arrives. What you get is a framework for building a plate, applied to your kitchen, your regional food and your week, so you can make the decision yourself in a restaurant or at your mother-in-law's house.

Do I need protein powder?

Usually not, and it is not something I prescribe. I can tell you what the research says about a category, and where food genuinely struggles to close a gap I will say so and send you to your doctor or pharmacist for the specifics. I take no commission and sell nothing.

Will I have to weigh my food forever?

No. You count for about three weeks so you learn what a portion looks like, then you stop. The tool above exists so that counting takes four minutes, not four weeks.
